# PRIV-001 — prediction-consensus privacy options

**Date:** 29 July 2026  
**Source branch:** PR #193 head `901a2bb92b74979283491e5c85d71b01657193a9`  
**Implementation status:** NOT YET IMPLEMENTED — owner decision required on PR #193

## Source findings

### Scope

`public.get_prediction_consensus(uuid)` is tournament-wide. Its cohort is every submitted Original Predictor entry for `p_tournament_id`. It accepts no league identifier, performs no league join and performs no league-membership check.

This is not the private-league post-lock reveal behaviour documented in `docs/design-system.md`. Tournament-wide disclosure therefore requires an explicit small-cohort privacy policy.

### Returned data

The JSON object contains:

- `server_now` and `locked_at`;
- `submitted_entries`;
- `champion_race`: up to ten `{team_id, picks}` rows;
- `peoples_final`: up to ten `{team_a_id, team_b_id, picks}` rows;
- `golden_boot`: up to ten `{player_id, team_id, picks}` rows;
- `most_agreed_match`: match/team identifiers, prediction count, home/draw/away counts, dominant outcome and dominant count;
- `most_divided_match`: match/team identifiers, prediction count, home/draw/away counts and the top-two count gap;
- `most_trusted_team`: `{team_id, predicted_wins}`;
- `goals_spread`: minimum, median, maximum, average, number of distinct totals and up to forty `{total_goals, entries}` buckets;
- `only_you`: up to six caller-specific unique champion, final, Golden Boot or exact-score cards.

It returns raw counts rather than percentages. It does not return a complete per-match breakdown or a most-popular exact scoreline.

### Existing eligibility

The function requires:

- an authenticated caller;
- an existing tournament;
- a configured tournament lock;
- current time at or after that lock;
- submitted entries for contributions to the aggregate.

It does not require the caller to have submitted an entry and does not require private-league membership.

### Inference risk

The response object is returned even when there are no submitted entries. One submitted entry is the smallest cohort producing substantive aggregate data. A caller whose own submitted entry contributes can subtract that known prediction from counts; at a cohort of two this can substantially disclose the other entrant. `only_you` also explicitly identifies caller selections whose count is one.

## Threshold options

| Option | Privacy | Product availability |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Minimum 5 submitted entries | Leaves as few as four other entries after a caller subtracts their own. Protects less, but suppresses less often in a small closed cohort. | Trends become available earlier. |
| Minimum 10 submitted entries | Leaves at least nine other entries after a contributing caller subtracts their own. This matches the conventional small-cell floor and materially reduces reconstruction risk. | Trends remain suppressed longer in small cohorts. |

## Suppressed response options

1. **Empty result:** ambiguous; the interface cannot distinguish suppression from genuinely absent prediction data.
2. **Explicit state:** return a successful JSON object such as `suppressed: true`, `reason: "not_enough_entries"`, `minimum_entries`, `submitted_entries`, `server_now` and `locked_at`. The client can present “Not enough entries yet” without treating privacy suppression as a failure.
3. **Database error:** rejected. It turns expected privacy behaviour into an application failure and encourages generic error handling.

## Caller counting

Recommended: count the caller's submitted entry toward the threshold.

This keeps the same tournament-wide result for every authenticated caller and avoids making suppression depend on whether the caller has an entry. At a threshold of 10, a contributing caller can remove their own prediction but still leaves a remainder of at least nine entries.

## Configuration options

| Model | Trade-off |
| --- | --- |
| Database constant | One auditable policy, simple pgTAP coverage and no weak per-competition setting. Changing it requires a reviewed migration. |
| Per-competition setting | Supports different cohort sizes but creates inconsistent privacy guarantees and requires an authoritative default/administration control. |
| Runtime configurable | Rejected for this baseline; it creates unnecessary mutable security policy and a new privilege/configuration surface. |

## Recommendation

Use a **database constant of 10 submitted entries, including the caller**, and return an **explicit successful suppression state** rather than empty data or an error.

Reasoning:

- the aggregate is tournament-wide rather than league-scoped;
- raw counts and caller uniqueness signals make subtraction practical;
- ten is the stronger conventional small-cell floor;
- an explicit state supports clear, non-error UI behaviour;
- a constant keeps privacy policy uniform and auditable during the Euro 2028 baseline.
