**Contract 202 — last Saturday reaches next Saturday's forecast (18 August 2026), applied to no hosted environment.** `ai/README.md` states the invariant: "the artefact that ships is then fitted fresh on every eligible completed match, so last Saturday reaches next Saturday's forecast." **Production was not meeting it.** Fifty-seven results from 14 to 17 August were imported at 05:55 on the 18th. `predict.py` then ran at 09:23 across the five leagues with upcoming fixtures, rebuilt team state from the full history — so its *features* did include the new results — scored all 52 fixtures, and wrote **nothing**: `{"written": 0, "fixtures": 12}` in every league. The cause is the identity, not the code around it. `predictions_one_per_model_fixture_horizon` is unique on `(model_id, fixture_id, horizon)` and `insert_predictions` is `on conflict do nothing`, which is correct — a prediction is immutable and a trigger enforces it. But `horizon` had one bucket, `scheduled`, covering everything beyond forty-eight hours, so a fixture eight days out was forecast once and every later forecast on better data collided with that row. The vocabulary gains `t72`, `t120` and `t168`: roughly six immutable forecasts across a week, each on strictly more completed football than the last. It cannot produce a second paper bet (contract 199's guard keys on the fixture) and cannot make a match count twice in a review (contract 201 collapses to the newest), and it finally gives contract 185's per-horizon report more than one horizon.

**`ai.canonical_fixture_predictions` gains meaningful tie-breaks in the same movement,** because a retrain writes a league in one transaction and several forecasts therefore share a `created_at` to the microsecond. The current model now outranks a retired one, and a later data snapshot outranks an earlier one, before the id is consulted.

**Contract 194 — `CUP-004`, a Championship tie asks whether each entrant may contest it (17 August 2026), applied to no hosted environment.** Counted over the installed `admin_settle_predictor_cup_round` — contract 98's, 289 lines — the terms `game_memberships`, `disqualif`, `withdraw` and `left_at` appear **zero** times. Settlement decided a knockout tie from whether each side submitted and how they scored, and never asked whether either could legally contest it. So an entrant who had been **disqualified** or who had **withdrawn**, but who submitted predictions before that happened, still won their tie on points and advanced through the bracket. `admin_disqualify_competition_game_entry` even writes `bonus_competition_entrants.outcome = 'eliminated'`, and the settlement driver read neither that nor the membership it came from. That is a correctness defect rather than a missing feature: the platform could already remove a player from a competition and then let them win a knockout tie.

**Eligibility is not submission, and keeping them apart is what makes the change safe.** "Neither entrant SUBMITTED" is an existing rule with an existing answer — `admin_walkover`, resolved by the better seed — and ADR 0022 forbids altering any qualification, seeding, bye, playoff-pairing or Penalty Number rule while rescoping this machinery, with ONE function serving both the tournament and the season. It is not changed. "Neither entrant may CONTEST the tie" had no rule because nothing ever asked, and that is the case ADR 0028 § 8 decides. The eligibility branch therefore sits strictly ABOVE the submission ladder, and `cupTieEligibilityParity.test.ts` proves the ladder is byte-identical by **round trip**: reversing the three insertions must reproduce contract 98's text exactly. That is contract 187's method, and it caught a one-newline error on its first run — which is the point of diffing rather than substring-checking.

**The behaviour was executed rather than reasoned about.** The matrix was run against the real function text over a stand-in of the thirteen relations it touches, with the away side deliberately outscoring the home side 20–10 so that points and eligibility disagree — a fixture in which the eligible player also scored more would pass against the old implementation. Both eligible: away wins on points, unchanged. No membership rows: unchanged, which is the deliberate fail-to-status-quo direction, because a missing row is an absence of evidence rather than evidence of ineligibility. Home withdrawn: away advances. **Away withdrawn: home advances, against the points.** Home disqualified: away advances. **Away disqualified: home advances, against the points.** Neither eligible: refused, nothing settled, no audit row. Re-running refuses with "This Cup round has already been settled", the settled winner is unchanged and no duplicate audit row appears.

**Where each ADR 0028 § 8 clause lands.** The eligible opponent advancing is the branch, with `decided_by = 'walkover'` inside the existing vocabulary — a new value such as `ineligible_opponent` was considered and rejected, because the ADR names audit evidence as where the reason belongs and widening a stored vocabulary it did not ask to widen would be a rule decision taken by a migration. The reason is a `bonus_competition_audit` row carrying the **stored** membership state rather than a paraphrase, plus explicit `invented_score: false` and `invented_points: false`. Nothing is written to any score, prediction or points relation. And "neither eligible" refuses the **whole window** rather than the one tie, which the existing all-or-nothing settled guard forces: a run that settled three ties and left a fourth open could never be re-driven, because the second run would find the three and refuse, stranding the fourth.

**One test detail is worth carrying forward rather than leaving in a diff.** The obvious leak assertion — search the payload for the opponent's value — was written as `not like '%84%'` and is unusable: a UUID is hexadecimal, so a bare two-digit search matches one about half the time and the assertion fails against a correct implementation. **Contract 192 — position over time, and one season-long rivalry (17 August 2026), applied to no hosted environment.** Two gaps, both measured on contract 191 rather than assumed. A weekly season could produce POINTS over time — every banked matchweek is in `season_matchweek_scores` — and could NOT produce POSITION over time: `get_season_league_rank_movement` (contract 150) answers "5th → 3rd" for ONE matchweek inside ONE private league, and `get_h2h_rank_history` (contract 48) is the tournament's, keyed on seven hard-coded matchday keys and reading `entry_totals`, which a competition season never writes. Those are different sentences about a player, and the difference is not cosmetic: somebody scoring steadily while the field scores faster is climbing on one chart and falling on the other, and the falling one is the true one. Separately, `get_season_head_to_head` (contract 129) compares two players over ONE named matchweek and returns their fixtures and picks — the right read for "what did we each predict last Saturday", and unable to answer "how do we compare" without being asked thirty-eight times, which is the browser loop the contract exists to prevent.

**It adds a second function that ranks a season, and that is the part worth reviewing rather than the reads on top of it.** The mitigation is that `predictor_internal.season_rank_history` reuses contract 94's ranking expression — `rank()`, so equal totals share a rank and the next rank SKIPS, where `dense_rank` would give 1, 2, 2, 3 against the authority's 1, 2, 2, 4 — and contract 94's whole field, read from `public.entries` so a player who has banked nothing is in the table on zero rather than missing from it. **The agreement is proved by running both authorities rather than by comparing their `order by` clauses.** At the last settled matchweek the cumulative total is by definition the season total, so `240_season_rank_history_and_rivalry.sql` requires the two to agree entry by entry. **The fixture is built so that assertion can fail:** measured on it, 6 distinct ranks across 12 players with a maximum rank of 12, against which `dense_rank` disagrees on 10 rows and `row_number` on 6. A first draft varied the score with both entry and matchweek, produced twelve distinct totals and zero ties, and the differential then passed against BOTH wrong implementations — that is recorded in the suite rather than quietly corrected, because a differential over a tie-free fixture is a test that cannot fail.

**One consequence is stated rather than hidden:** reusing the canonical field means a player who joined at matchweek 10 appears in the field at matchweek 3 on zero points. Excluding an entry from matchweeks before it existed would be a SECOND interpretation of who is in the table and would make this function disagree with the standings it is measured against, so the honest answer is the canonical field with `matchweeksPlayed` reported beside it — which is what ADR 0012 pairs with points for exactly this reason.

**`get_season_rivalry` answers a whole comparison in one request** — both identities, rank, points, matchweeks played, exact-score and correct-outcome counts, the head-to-head matchweek record, recent form and the signed points gap. It counts a matchweek only when BOTH players banked it: the easy implementation folds a missing row to zero and manufactures a 12–0 record against somebody who joined in February, and `matchweeksCompared` is returned beside the record so the number is never read without its denominator. Neither read returns an individual prediction, both take their permission from contract 191's single visibility authority, and the rivalry read speaks only about matchweeks that are settled AND past their own lock — reusing contract 129's choice to hide on a NULL lock, which costs a settled matchweek in an unpublished calendar and buys the property that the two comparison reads cannot disagree about whether a matchweek is open.

## Provider-ingestion authority boundary

Contract 96 is custody, not promotion:

- the Edge Function accepts only named, fixed providers and bounded relative paths;
- the caller key is checked before any provider I/O;
- exact raw response text is archived before parse or strict decode;
- processing attempts are append-only evidence;
- browser roles receive no relation or RPC access;
- safe response headers are allowlisted and response size is bounded;
- credential-shaped query parameters are rejected;
- no provider path may write official fixtures, results, lock state, points, totals, ranks or standings.

No provider credential or provider request is part of contract 96 delivery.

**Retention is permitted (owner confirmation, 5 August 2026).** All three providers allow their responses to be stored. This is recorded because it was the blocking question rather than a formality: the custody design archives exact raw response text and keeps it, so a licence permitting the call but not the retention would have made the control itself the breach — and it would have been discovered only after the data was stored. Coverage, timezone and exceptional-state mappings are still open; those are measured, not asserted, by `scripts/ops/provider-bakeoff.ts`.

**The identity map exists at contract 112, and is applied to development.** `public.provider_entity_map` relates a provider's season, round and team identifiers to this platform's rows, always within one competition season. It is the map alone: it resolves, and it reports which identifiers in a payload are still unmapped and how many fixtures each one blocks. It imports nothing.

The scoping is a composite foreign key rather than a convention — `(tournament_id, team_id)` — so the database itself refuses to map a provider identifier onto a club in a different competition season. That mistake is otherwise entirely silent: the fixtures produced would be plausible and wrong, and what anyone would see is a league table that is subtly untrue rather than an error.

**Why the map and nothing else.** Building it found the reason. `src/domain/season/fixtureReassignment.ts` resolves a moved kickoff by ROUND WINDOW, and `competition_rounds` had no window columns. Inventing one inside an ingestion migration would decide a lock-adjacent rule as a side effect of plumbing, so it got its own contract.

**The round play window is contract 113, applied to development.** Each round carries the inclusive span it is played over, derived from its own fixtures, with a trigger keeping windows disjoint within a competition season. The bounds are inclusive at both ends to match the domain module's containment test, so windows that merely *touch* are refused — an instant on a shared boundary would fall in both rounds, which is precisely the ambiguity being excluded. It is stored rather than computed at resolve time because the derivation reads fixtures and reassignment moves one: computing at the moment of the question would make the answer depend on it.

**The decision that followed, and what it costs contract 113.** Contract 113 was built to answer "which round does this kickoff fall in", because [ADR 0020](../adr/0020-football-prediction-hub-product-model.md) §79 reassigns a rescheduled fixture to that round and never defines *falls in*. The owner amendment of 5 August 2026 **reverses that section**: a rescheduled fixture is not reassigned at all. It stays in the matchweek it was scheduled in, its prediction stays editable until its own kickoff, and a single moved match never creates a round. **The round is a label, not a position:** the fixture still scores into its original matchweek, but wherever fixtures are listed by date it appears at its actual kickoff in true chronological order, carrying its original matchweek as its label. Grouping strictly by round is the comfortable misreading, and it would put a November match under a September heading above matches played weeks earlier.

That is a simpler rule and mostly existing machinery — ADR 0020 already names the per-match guard as the integrity floor, and Stage C1's lock enforcement already compares against each fixture's current kickoff, so the amendment promotes that guard rather than introducing anything. It also removes a class of question outright: no boundary between matchweeks has to be chosen, and points cannot move between matchweeks after the fact.

**It also removes contract 113's stated consumer, and that is recorded rather than glossed.** The play window is still a true fact — it is when a round is played — but reassignment no longer asks the question it was built to answer. Either it finds a justified use or it is recorded as unused capability; what it must not do is keep a migration header claiming a purpose the amendment removed.

**What is still missing between a provider response and a fixture.** Nothing writes `season_fixtures` — the Stage C1 foundation says so in terms, deferring "the ingestion and administrative reassignment workflow" to a later contract. The shape it takes is decided **by change class**, in [ADR 0023 § Development competition administration](../adr/0023-hub-information-architecture.md#development-competition-administration): an existing correctly mapped fixture's kickoff may be revised automatically (`INGEST-001`), while a newly discovered fixture, a removal, a cancellation, an abandonment or a material identity or round change each require administrative approval (`INGEST-002`, `INGEST-003`). Ambiguity fails closed (`INGEST-004`), and provider data never becomes official result truth automatically (`INGEST-006`) — official scoring and progression stay governed by the existing fail-closed result rules.

This paragraph previously restated ADR 0020's earlier wording — "fixture changes import automatically and notify an administrator for review" — as though it were one rule for every change class. It is not, and reading it that way would authorise a provider to create and delete this platform's fixtures. The boundary is stated once, in the decision, and linked from here rather than copied.

- **`/auth/signup` no longer bypasses the publication state on the Euro build.** PR #702 removed the landing page's "Create account" control in the states where the server says registration is closed and left the route serving a working form, so a bookmark, a shared link, a search result or a typed URL created a real account for a tournament nobody had published. `EuroSignupGate` refuses at the route, reads the same `registrationOpen` table the control reads so the two cannot disagree, fails closed on a failed read, and is a **no-op on the Hub build** — one Supabase project serves both deployments, so a project-level signup switch would have closed weekly signup to close this one. - **The domestic weekly tree is behind a deployment boundary.** `/competitions` and every `/competitions/:competitionSlug/:seasonSlug/**` surface are the Hub's product, and the Euro build was serving all of them; a catalogue omission is not a control, because a guessable URL still resolves. `servesDomesticCompetitions` is the mirror of `servesEuroTournament` and, unlike it, is already false where it should be — withdrawing the weekly tree from the Euro build strands no browser evidence, because no journey enters it from there. The desktop rail's competitions group, which lists domestic seasons and ends in the weekly catalogue, no longer renders under a heading reading "Tournament"; and first sign-in no longer walks a tournament visitor through four domestic onboarding steps.

Match Predictor is not a game you attach to something — it IS a competition season, and the seasons it runs over are the Premier League and the Scottish Premiership. Listed under Euro 2028 it promised a weekly domestic game on a tournament site with nowhere to send anyone who wanted it. It is now ranked `elsewhere`: named so a player looking for it is not left to conclude it does not exist, and pointed at the Hub, and only where the Hub's origin is configured.

**Contract 214 — current value follows the current forecast (23 August 2026), repository only.** A fixture deliberately accumulates immutable forecast horizons as evidence improves. `find_value.py` previously allowed every valid historical horizon to compete for today's betting decision, while the Lab's fixture read displayed only the canonical newest forecast; separately, `ai.current_fixture_recommendations` could continue exposing an older recommendation after a fresher forecast arrived but before value was recalculated.
