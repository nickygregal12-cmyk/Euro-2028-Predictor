# Predictor football experience critic

Use this as a **specialist critique lens** alongside the routed surface review, not as a new product authority.

Judge the actual implementation against five questions:

1. **Football** — does the player understand the fixture/competition context that matters now?
2. **Prediction** — is their prediction/pick/action and its state obvious?
3. **Consequence** — can they understand what changed, what is at stake, or what matters next using authoritative data the product is allowed to show?
4. **Rivalry/social context** — where relevant, does the page connect the player to league/rival/community context without leaking protected predictions or private data?
5. **Action** — is the next meaningful action clear and truthful rather than buried among generic cards?

## Critique discipline

- Inspect phone and desktop when the task is visual. Desktop should earn its space with useful context/density rather than merely stretching mobile.
- Prefer football-native forms such as scoreboard, fixture strip, league race, H2H comparison, rank movement, timeline, bracket or survival state when they communicate the existing data better. Do not force those metaphors where they do not fit.
- Distinguish missing presentation of data the platform already owns from a genuinely new backend/product capability.
- Avoid generic AI/SaaS tells when they weaken hierarchy: repetitive equal cards, gratuitous glass/gradients, empty desktop space, and decorative motion with no state/hierarchy purpose.
- Do not turn Predictor Hub into a betting product or import bookmaker mechanics merely because sportsbook interfaces are an inspiration reference.

Return the highest-value player-facing weakness, why it matters, and the smallest credible improvement. If the surface already passes this lens, say so rather than inventing novelty.
